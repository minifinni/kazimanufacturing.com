Nepal vs Bangladesh: Where Should UK Fashion Brands Manufacture?

# Nepal vs Bangladesh: Where Should UK Fashion Brands Manufacture?

Choosing between Nepal and Bangladesh for garment manufacturing is one of the most consequential decisions UK fashion brands face. While Bangladesh dominates headlines with its massive export volumes, Nepal offers distinct advantages that can make it the smarter choice for certain business models. Let's break down the numbers that actually matter.

Cost Comparison: Beyond the Unit Price

Bangladesh remains the cheaper option on paper. Basic t-shirts run $2.50-$4.00 FOB (Free On Board), compared to Nepal's $3.50-$5.50 for comparable quality. For a UK brand ordering 5,000 units, that's a $5,000-$7,500 difference upfront.

But the math changes when you factor in hidden costs:

  • Bangladesh minimum wages: £75/month (2024 rates)
  • Nepal minimum wages: £95-£120/month depending on skill level

The wage gap narrows when you account for productivity. Nepali factories average 8-12% higher efficiency rates on complex garments due to more experienced operators. For detailed embroidery, appliqué work, or intricate cuts, Nepal's labour cost disadvantage evaporates.

Minimum Order Quantities: The Startup Factor

This is where Nepal shines for emerging UK brands:

| Product Type | Bangladesh MOQ | Nepal MOQ | |-------------|----------------|-----------| | Basic t-shirts | 3,000-5,000 units | 500-1,000 units | | Tailored shirts | 1,500-2,000 units | 300-500 units | | Outerwear | 1,000+ units | 200-500 units | | Accessories | 2,000+ units | 500 units |

For a UK startup testing market fit, Nepal's flexibility is invaluable. You can run three 500-unit colourways for the price commitment Bangladesh demands for a single 3,000-unit run.

Lead Times: Speed to Market

Bangladesh: 60-90 days from order confirmation to UK warehouse Nepal: 45-65 days for most garments

Nepal wins on speed for several reasons:

  1. Smaller factory queues and less production backlog
  2. Proximity to Indian textile hubs (many fabrics sourced from Surat/Noida)
  3. Simpler logistics chain with fewer intermediaries

For UK brands operating on fast-fashion or seasonal cycles, those 15-25 days can mean capturing full-price sales versus mid-season discounting.

Quality & Specialisation

Bangladesh excels at high-volume basics: denim, t-shirts, underwear. The infrastructure supports massive scale, and quality is consistent at the mid-market level.

Nepal dominates in:

  • Technical outerwear (Gore-Tex, insulated jackets)
  • Artisanal techniques (hand-embroidery, block printing, traditional weaving)
  • Small-batch luxury production
  • Organic and natural dye processes

UK outdoor brands like Sherpa Adventure Gear and smaller sustainable labels have made Nepal their base precisely for this technical capability.

Ethics & Compliance: The Real Story

Bangladesh's garment sector has improved significantly since Rana Plaza (2013), with 93% of export factories now accredited by safety alliances. However, compliance costs have pushed minimum orders even higher—factories can't justify audit overhead on sub-1,000 unit runs.

Nepal's smaller scale means:

  • Easier factory audits and relationship-based accountability
  • Higher traceability (you can often visit the actual production floor)
  • Stronger worker retention (lower turnover = better craftsmanship)
  • ILO compliance with less bureaucratic overhead

For UK brands marketing transparency, Nepal offers authentic storytelling opportunities that Bangladesh's scale makes difficult.

UK Import Considerations

Tariffs: Both countries enjoy duty-free access to the UK under the Developing Countries Trading Scheme (DCTS), provided goods meet rules of origin requirements.

Shipping costs: Bangladesh benefits from established sea freight routes—£1,200-£1,800 for a 20ft container to UK ports. Nepal requires overland transport to Kolkata or Visakhapatnam (5-7 days) before sea freight, adding £300-£500 per container in logistics costs.

Carbon footprint: Nepal's route via India adds roughly 15-20% to shipping emissions. For carbon-conscious UK brands, this is a consideration—offset by Nepal's stronger renewable energy mix (hydroelectric powers 90%+ of garment production).

The Verdict

Choose Bangladesh if: You're running high-volume basics, have established UK distribution, and price-per-unit is your primary metric. The infrastructure and economies of scale are unmatched.

Choose Nepal if: You're building a brand with values-driven positioning, need flexible MOQs for testing, produce technical or artisanal garments, or prioritise speed-to-market over absolute lowest cost.

The sweet spot? Many successful UK brands use both: Bangladesh for high-volume core lines, Nepal for capsule collections, limited editions, and premium positioning.

The manufacturing decision isn't just about cost—it's about aligning production capabilities with brand strategy. Nepal offers UK founders something Bangladesh can't easily replicate: agility.
