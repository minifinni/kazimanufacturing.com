# The True Cost of Manufacturing in Nepal: A 2025 Breakdown

UK ecommerce operators often underestimate what it actually costs to bring a Nepali-manufactured product to market. The FOB price on your factory invoice is just the starting point. Here's the complete cost anatomy for 2025, with real numbers you can use for financial modelling.

Case Study: The £12 Landed T-Shirt

Let's follow a typical organic cotton t-shirt from a Kathmandu factory to a UK customer's doorstep, then trace how it reaches an £85 retail price point.

Production Costs (Nepal)

| Cost Component | Amount (USD) | Notes | |---------------|--------------|-------| | Raw fabric (organic cotton jersey) | $2.80/m | 1.2m per t-shirt = $3.36 | | Trims (labels, threads, buttons) | $0.45 | Recycled paper labels, organic cotton threads | | Labour (cut, sew, finish) | $1.20 | 45 minutes labour at $1.60/hour | | Factory overhead & profit | $0.85 | 15-20% markup | | FOB Kathmandu | $5.86 | ~£4.65 at current rates |

The FOB price is what most factory quotes show. But your actual cost begins climbing from here.

Shipping & Logistics

Inland transport (Kathmandu to Kolkata): $0.35/unit

  • 1,000km overland journey through India
  • 5-7 days transit time
  • Includes customs documentation at border

Sea freight (Kolkata to Felixstowe): $0.42/unit

  • Part of a shared 20ft container
  • 18-22 days sailing time
  • Includes UK port handling

UK inland delivery to warehouse: $0.18/unit

  • Final mile from port to your 3PL or storage

Shipping subtotal: $0.95/unit (~£0.75)

UK Import Costs

Import VAT (20%): £1.08

  • Calculated on (CIF value + duty)
  • CIF = Cost + Insurance + Freight = ~£5.40
  • Payment due at UK border or deferred via PVA (Postponed VAT Accounting)

Import duty: £0.00

  • Nepal qualifies for duty-free access under UK DCTS (Developing Countries Trading Scheme)
  • Rules of origin: minimum 40% value-added in Nepal (easily met)

Customs clearance & agency fees: £0.35/unit

  • Broker fees, documentation, handling

Import costs subtotal: £1.43/unit

Warehousing & Fulfilment

3PL receiving & storage: £0.25/unit

  • Unloading, barcode scanning, shelf storage (per month)

Pick & pack: £1.20/order

  • Single-item order, standard packaging
  • Includes packaging materials (mailer bag, invoice)

Fulfilment subtotal: £1.45/unit (assuming single-item orders)

The Full Landed Cost Calculation

| Stage | Cost per Unit | |-------|---------------| | FOB Kathmandu | £4.65 | | Shipping to UK | £0.75 | | Import VAT & clearance | £1.43 | | Warehousing & fulfilment | £1.45 | | Total Landed Cost | £8.28 |

Note: Our headline £12 figure includes a 45% buffer for currency fluctuation, quality defects (typically 2-3%), and returns processing. Conservative planning protects margins.

From £12 to £85: The Margin Architecture

Now the commercial reality. How does a £12 landed t-shirt sell for £85?

| Component | Amount | % of Retail | |-----------|--------|-------------| | Landed cost | £12.00 | 14.1% | | Payment processing (2.9% + £0.20) | £2.67 | 3.1% | | Marketing/customer acquisition | £17.00 | 20.0% | | Returns allowance (15% rate) | £3.60 | 4.2% | | Operational overhead | £8.50 | 10.0% | | Total costs | £43.77 | 51.5% | | Gross profit | £41.23 | 48.5% | | Gross margin | — | 48.5% |

The 2.5x markup from landed cost to retail (£12 → £85) is standard in sustainable fashion. Premium positioning justifies this through brand storytelling, not just product quality.

Where Costs Can Be Optimised

1. Consolidate Shipments

Shipping costs drop 30-40% when ordering 2,000+ units versus 500. The break-even analysis:

  • 500 units: £0.95/unit shipping
  • 2,000 units: £0.58/unit shipping
  • 5,000 units: £0.42/unit shipping

Savings on a 2,000-unit order: £740. Enough to fund a second production run.

2. Negotiate Incoterms

Switching from FOB to DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) often makes sense for smaller brands:

  • Factory handles shipping, customs, and delivery to your door
  • Predictable costs: add 12-15% to FOB price
  • Eliminates surprise customs charges and clearance delays
  • Cash flow benefit: pay factory on 30-60 day terms versus immediate VAT at border

3. Direct-to-Consumer Model

Every middleman erodes margin. Compare:

  • Wholesale: Landed cost £12 → wholesale £30 (60% margin) → retail £75 (brand captures £18/unit)
  • DTC: Landed cost £12 → retail £65 (81% gross margin, £53/unit after payment processing)

The DTC model generates 2.9x more revenue per unit. That's why direct brands can afford Nepali manufacturing while wholesale-dependent competitors source from Bangladesh or Vietnam.

4. Returns Management

A 15% return rate destroys margins. Each returned t-shirt costs:

  • Initial shipping: £3.50
  • Processing & inspection: £1.20
  • Re-stocking or disposal: £0.80
  • Total return cost: £5.50

Reducing return rates from 15% to 8% (through better size guides, fabric descriptions, and photography) saves £1.43 per unit sold—equivalent to your entire import VAT cost.

5. Payment Terms Negotiation

Standard Nepali factory terms: 30% deposit, 70% against shipping documents.

Negotiate for established relationships:

  • 50/50 split on repeat orders
  • 30/40/30 on orders over $10,000 (deposit, mid-production, pre-shipment)
  • Net-30 or Net-60 terms after 3+ successful orders

Better terms improve cash flow more than marginal FOB reductions.

The Hidden Cost: Time

Not captured in spreadsheets but critical to ecommerce operators: Nepal's 45-65 day lead time versus 90+ days from Bangladesh or 30 days from Portugal.

The opportunity cost of stockouts often exceeds the savings from faster/cheaper sourcing. For UK operators running lean inventory, Nepal hits the sweet spot of reasonable cost with manageable lead times.

Bottom Line

Nepali manufacturing isn't the cheapest option. But for UK ecommerce operators building sustainable, values-driven brands, the total cost structure works. A £12 landed cost supports £65-85 retail pricing with healthy 45-50% margins—provided you control the variables above.

The brands winning with Nepali manufacturing aren't optimising for the lowest FOB price. They're optimising for the lowest total cost of serving their specific customer, including brand differentiation that commands premium pricing.

That's the calculation that matters.
