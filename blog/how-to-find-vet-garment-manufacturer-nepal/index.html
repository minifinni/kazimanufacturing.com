How to Find and Vet a Garment Manufacturer in Nepal: A UK Brand's Complete Guide

2026-03-10  ·  nepal, manufacturing, sourcing, vetting, due-diligence, uk-brands

Finding a garment manufacturer in Nepal isn't the hard part. Finding one that won't waste your time, money, or reputation is where most UK brands stumble.

This guide covers exactly how to source, vet, and validate Nepali manufacturers—from first Google search to first production run.

Where to Actually Find Nepal Garment Manufacturers

Trade directories (verified):

  • Nepal Trade Integration Strategy (NTIS) maintains an exporter database
  • FNCCI (Federation of Nepalese Chambers of Commerce and Industry) member lists
  • Handloom Association Nepal for knitwear specialists

Direct outreach that works:

LinkedIn searches for "garment export Nepal" or "apparel manufacturer Kathmandu" yield factory owners and export managers with active profiles. Unlike Bangladesh or China, Nepali manufacturers often handle their own outreach—no thick layers of trading companies to navigate.

What to avoid:

Alibaba has limited Nepal presence. Most listings are traders, not factories. Stick to Nepal-specific export directories or direct LinkedIn contact.

The Vetting Checklist: 12 Questions Before You Visit

Ask these before booking flights to Kathmandu:

  1. "What's your monthly capacity in units?"
    • Under 5,000 units/month: Small workshop, good for sampling
    • 10,000–50,000 units/month: Mid-tier factory, suitable for growing brands
    • Over 50,000 units/month: Large exporter, high MOQs
  2. "Who are your current UK/EU clients?"
    • Legitimate factories will share anonymised portfolio details
    • Red flag: "We can't disclose due to NDAs" (unusual for Nepal)
  3. "Can you send photos of your production floor and cutting tables?"
    • Request timestamped photos or a short video call
    • Check for clean floors, organised fabric storage, adequate lighting
  4. "What's your cutting-to-shipping lead time for a 500-unit order?"
    • Nepal average: 45–60 days for knits, 60–75 days for woven
    • Under 30 days: Likely outsourcing to subcontractors
  5. "Do you have in-house pattern making and sampling?"
    • Critical for brands without technical teams
    • Outsourced sampling adds 2–3 weeks and communication friction
  6. "What certifications do you hold?"
    • WRAP, SA8000, or GOTS for organic
    • Many small Nepali factories lack certs but maintain ethical practices—judge on evidence, not just paperwork
  7. "Can you handle fabric sourcing, or do we need to supply?"
    • Local fabric options in Nepal are limited (mostly cotton knits, some bamboo)
    • Factories sourcing from India/China add 10–15 days but widen options
  8. "What's your defect rate, and how do you handle rejects?"
    • Industry standard: 2–3% AQL (Acceptable Quality Level)
    • Good factories will explain their QC process, not just quote a number
  9. "Do you offer CIF or FOB terms?"
    • FOB Kathmandu/TIA: You handle freight (more control)
    • CIF UK port: Factory arranges shipping (simpler for small brands)
  10. "Can we arrange a third-party inspection before shipping?"
    • Factories resistant to independent inspection are a hard pass
    • SGS, Bureau Veritas, or local inspectors like Nepal Inspection Services
  11. "What's your payment structure?"
    • Standard: 30% deposit, 70% before shipping
    • Demanding 50%+ upfront or full payment before production starts: Red flag
  12. "Are you the direct manufacturer, or do you subcontract?"
    • Subcontracting isn't bad, but you need transparency
    • Hidden subcontracting = quality and compliance risks

Red Flags Specific to Nepal

"We can do any quantity"

Nepal's garment sector is small-batch friendly, but no factory excels at everything. A manufacturer claiming MOQs from 50 to 50,000 units with consistent quality is likely brokering orders.

Vague location details

Kathmandu Valley holds 80%+ of Nepal's garment exporters. Factories claiming to be "in the mountains" or refusing specific location details often operate from informal workshops with inconsistent standards.

No export licence number

Every legitimate Nepali garment exporter registers with the Department of Commerce and holds an export licence. Ask for the number—it's public record.

Pressure to pay via informal channels

Wire to a corporate account in Nepal. If asked to pay a "manager" personally or use informal remittance, walk away.

First Contact Email Template

Subject: Manufacturing Partnership Enquiry — [Your Brand] UK

Hi [Name],

I'm [Your Name], founder of [Brand], a UK clothing brand specialising in [product type]. We're currently manufacturing [X units/year] and exploring Nepal as a sourcing destination for our [specific product].

Before arranging a visit, I'd appreciate details on:

1. Current capacity and typical order sizes
2. Experience with UK/EU brands (if any)
3. Lead times for [product type] from sampling to shipping
4. Your payment and shipping terms

We're particularly interested in [specific requirement — e.g., organic cotton, specific certifications, small-batch flexibility].

Could you share some recent photos of your facility and any relevant certifications?

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Brand]
[Website]
[LinkedIn profile]

Site Visit: What to Check in Person

If the email responses look solid, book a trip. Kathmandu to most factory locations is under 90 minutes.

On the factory floor:

  • Are workers wearing protective equipment (masks for cutting, guards on machines)?
  • Is fabric stored off the floor, away from moisture?
  • Do they show you their actual cutting tables and sewing lines, or just a showroom?

Office and documentation:

  • Export licence visible on the wall (legally required)
  • Fire safety equipment present and accessible
  • Payroll records if you're verifying fair wages

Sampling capability:

  • Ask them to walk you through a recent sample they made
  • Check pattern-making equipment (CAD systems vs. hand drafting)

Cost Benchmarks for Validation

Use these figures to sanity-check quotes:

Item Nepal Typical Range UK Comparison
Plain t-shirt (mid-weight organic cotton) £4.50–£6.50 FOB £12–£18 manufactured UK
Hoodie (320gsm fleece-lined) £8.50–£12.00 FOB £18–£28 manufactured UK
Sampling cost per style £75–£150 £200–£400 UK-based
Air freight to UK (per kg) £4.50–£6.50 N/A
Sea freight LCL (per CBM) £85–£120 N/A

Quotes significantly under these ranges suggest corner-cutting on labour or materials. Overly high quotes often indicate the factory is outsourcing to a more expensive subcontractor.

Building the Relationship

Nepali business culture values personal relationships. The factory owner who meets you at the airport, shows you their family photos, and insists on tea is often more reliable than the slick operation with a glossy brochure.

That said: friendliness doesn't replace due diligence. Trust your checklist first, the relationship second.

Next Steps

  1. Shortlist 3–5 factories using the directory sources above
  2. Send the email template, adapted to your product
  3. Score responses against the 12-question checklist
  4. Visit your top 2 before committing to production
  5. Start with a small test order (100–250 units) before scaling

Nepal's garment sector punches above its weight on craftsmanship and ethics. The factories worth working with are there—you just need to filter carefully to find them.
